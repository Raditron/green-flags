# Bulgarian legal basis for the beach green/yellow/red flag system

Research date: 2026-08-05

## TL;DR

There **is** a real, currently-in-force Bulgarian regulation that defines the beach flag
colors and mandates their use. It does **not** contain any codified numeric physical
threshold (wave height, wind speed/Beaufort, current speed) for when a lifeguard must
switch from green to yellow to red. The choice of flag on any given day is left entirely
to the professional judgment of the on-duty lifeguard / chief lifeguard
("спасител-ръководител"), informally guided by a wind/wave reference scale that stations
are required to keep on hand but that is not itself part of the binding legal text. Any
"the red flag means >X m waves / force Y wind" claim you may see in tourist blogs is not
sourced from this regulation and should be treated as folk knowledge, not law.

## The governing instrument

**Наредба за водноспасителната дейност и обезопасяването на водните площи и басейните за
обществено ползване** ("Ordinance on Water-Rescue Activity and the Safety of Water Areas
and Public-Use Pools").

- Adopted by the Council of Ministers, **Постановление № 82 от 03.04.2024 г.**
- Promulgated **Държавен вестник (State Gazette) бр. 30 от 5 април 2024 г.**
- Amended by Постановление № 188/30.05.2024, promulgated ДВ бр. 47 от 4 юни 2024 г. (in
  force 04.06.2024)
- Legal basis: **чл. 78а, ал. 2 от Закона за здравето** (Article 78a(2) of the Health
  Act) — i.e. it is a Ministry of Health-sponsored act, adopted as a Council of Ministers
  decree, not a ministerial order.
- It **repealed** the previous regulation of the same subject, Постановление № 182 от
  1996 г. (обн. ДВ бр. 65/1996, various amendments through 2020), which had no numeric
  criteria either (see "History" below).

## Who sets it, who enforces it, who executes it (day to day)

This is spread across several actors — there is no single "flag authority":

1. **Council of Ministers** (on Ministry of Health's proposal) — sets the rules (the
   ordinance itself).
2. **Concessionaire or lessee of the sea beach** ("стопанин на морски плаж" — the private
   or municipal operator holding the concession/lease) — is the party legally responsible
   for organizing water-rescue activity and for actually raising/lowering flags (чл. 4,
   чл. 8, чл. 27).
3. **Minister of Tourism** — must approve, by 31 March each year, the concessionaire's/
   lessee's seasonal "water-rescue activity program," including the map of which stretches
   of water get "maximum safety" coverage, lifeguard-post placement, etc. (чл. 27(1)-(2)).
4. **Изпълнителна агенция "Морска администрация" (Maritime Administration Executive
   Agency)**, via its Varna maritime rescue and coordination center — must sign off on the
   guarded-water-area scheme before the Minister of Tourism approves it, and is the escalation
   point for incidents more than 200 m from shore (чл. 13(2), чл. 14(7), чл. 27(2)).
5. **Областен управител (regional governor)** — can order ("предписание") that a given
   water area/stretch be secured, even beaches otherwise unguarded (чл. 3(1)(3), чл. 6(3),
   чл. 7(4)).
6. **Bulgarian Red Cross (Български Червен кръст)** — runs lifeguard training/certification
   (per a Ministry-of-Health-approved curriculum) and the mandatory annual fitness/skills
   re-certification of every lifeguard (чл. 31-32, Приложение № 8). ILS-certified foreign
   lifeguards are also recognized (чл. 31(6)).
7. **On-duty lifeguard / chief lifeguard ("спасител" / "спасител-ръководител")** — the
   person who actually decides, hour to hour, which flag is flying, based on their own
   assessment of conditions ("хидрометеорологичните условия"), which they are required to
   log in the post diary (чл. 33(1)(8)) but which the ordinance does not further constrain
   numerically.
8. **Ministry of Interior (МВР)** — can be called in by a lifeguard to enforce compliance
   against beachgoers who ignore instructions (чл. 33(2)); disobeying a raised red flag is
   treated as an administrative offense subject to fines (this practice is widely reported
   in Bulgarian press, though the fine amounts/legal basis for the administrative-offense
   sanction itself sit in a different act, not fully reproduced in the sources I reviewed).
9. **Регионална здравна инспекция (RZI, Regional Health Inspectorate)** — regional-level
   bodies under the Ministry of Health; several RZIs (e.g. RZI Burgas, RZI Dobrich)
   publicize the ordinance and are commonly cited by journalists as the "face" of beach
   safety enforcement locally, but the ordinance's own text does not name RZI as the body
   that inspects or approves individual flag decisions — its clearest role in this regime
   is bathing-water-*quality* monitoring under the separate Наредба № 5/2008 (see below),
   which the ordinance cross-references as a precondition for a beach qualifying for
   "maximum safety" (flag-eligible) status (чл. 7(2)). I could not find a provision giving
   RZI day-to-day authority over flag-raising itself — treat any claim that "RZI decides
   the flag color" as unconfirmed by the primary text.

Water *quality* (as opposed to wave/current safety) is governed by a **separate**
instrument referenced in чл. 7(2): **Наредба № 5 от 2008 г. за управление качеството на
водите за къпане** (ДВ, бр. 53/2008) — "Ordinance No. 5/2008 on Management of Bathing
Water Quality." A beach must meet that ordinance's water-quality requirements before it
can even be classified for "maximum safety" (i.e., flag-eligible, lifeguard-staffed)
status. I did not do a deep-dive into Naredba No. 5/2008's own text in this pass (it's a
distinct regulatory track from wave/flag safety and would be a separate research task if
GreenFlags needs Blue-Flag/bathing-water-quality thresholds too — those do have EU-derived
numeric parameters, unlike the flag-color rule).

## What the ordinance actually says about the flags

Flag colors/shapes/meanings are defined in **Приложение № 2 (Annex 2), to чл. 7, ал. 1 и
3** of the ordinance — this is a table, reproduced here verbatim (Pantone codes and
physical flag dimensions omitted where not relevant):

| # | Color | Meaning (official Bulgarian text) | English gloss |
|---|-------|-----------------------------------|----------------|
| 1 | Зелен (Green) | "Къпането разрешено" | Swimming permitted |
| 2 | Жълт (Yellow) | "Къпането разрешено, но с повишено внимание. Забранено влизането във водата с плаващи и надуваеми предмети" | Swimming permitted but with increased caution. Entering the water with floats/inflatables is forbidden |
| 3 | Червен (Red) | "Къпането забранено" | Swimming forbidden |
| 4 | Червено-жълто (Red-yellow, horizontal bicolor) | "Начало и край на максимално обезопасената брегова ивица" | Marks the start/end of the "maximum safety" (lifeguard-covered) shoreline stretch — a *boundary* marker, not a hazard-level flag |
| 5 | Черно-бяло (Black-white checkered) | "Зона за влизане и излизане на плавателни съдове за развлекателни цели" | Marks the entry/exit lane for recreational watercraft (e.g. surfers/jet-skis), not a hazard-level flag |

That is the **entire** legal content on flag meaning. Article 7(1) and 7(3) just say that
"maximum safety" areas must display flag signaling "according to Annex 2" and that the
boundary of the max-safety zone is marked with the red-yellow flag/sign. Article 23(2)
requires every max-safety water area to post an information board explaining "the flag
signaling per Annex 2" to visitors. Article 36(2) obliges visitors to "comply with the
flag signaling." Nowhere in the 36 articles or 8 annexes is there a definition of the
physical/meteorological conditions (wave height, wind force, current speed, storm
warning, jellyfish, visibility, etc.) that should trigger green vs. yellow vs. red.

### What is explicitly NOT numerically defined
- No wave-height threshold (in meters or Beaufort sea-state) for any flag color.
- No wind-speed threshold (Beaufort or m/s) for any flag color.
- No rip-current / undertow numeric criterion for flag color (undertow is mentioned only
  qualitatively, e.g. чл. 14(6): bottom pits, underwater rocks/currents, whirlpools are to
  be marked with red floating buoys and warning signs on shore — a static hazard marker,
  not a daily flag-color trigger).
- No storm/lightning-warning-linked flag rule.
- No jellyfish-linked flag rule.
- No visibility-linked flag rule.
- No explicit water-quality-linked *daily* flag rule (water quality gates whether a beach
  qualifies for "maximum safety"/staffed status at all, under the separate Naredba
  No. 5/2008, but doesn't map to a same-day flag color choice in this ordinance).

### What IS explicitly, numerically defined (but not about flag color)
These are the only hard numbers in the whole ordinance, and none of them drive flag
selection:
- Children's supervised swimming area (чл. 34(2)): water depth 0.70–1.50 m, current speed
  ≤ 0.5 m/s, 2–4 m² of water surface per child, boundary marked with yellow floating
  markers spaced 10–15 m apart; nearby hazards (undertow, unsafe bottom) marked with red
  floating markers.
- Open-canal safety ladders required when water speed > 1.5 m/s (чл. 6(5)).
- Rescue-post staffing ratios (chapter III): e.g. ≥1 lifeguard post per 100 m of shoreline
  in "national resorts," ≤150 m elsewhere (чл. 9(1)); ≥2 lifeguards per beach post (чл.
  10(1)); a rescue station required per 5 posts (чл. 11(1)); medical-crew scaling by beach
  length in bands of ≤200 m / 201–1000 m / 1001–2000 m / ≥2001 m (чл. 17(1)).
- Water-rescue duty season/hours: 1 June – 30 September, 09:00–18:30 daily (чл. 26(1)) —
  i.e. **outside this window there is no mandated lifeguard coverage or flag display at
  all**, which matters for an app trying to predict "today's flag."
- Lifeguard minimum age 18 (17 while still in training), annual Red-Cross fitness
  recertification with specific swim-time standards (Приложение № 8): e.g. beach
  lifeguards must swim 100 m freestyle in 1:40 and complete a 25 m swim + 25 m tow rescue
  in 1:10.

### Indirect evidence that flag choice is discretionary, not ruleset-driven
- Чл. 33(1)(8): the lifeguard's daily post log must record "хидрометеорологичните
  условия" (weather/sea conditions) each day — logged as an observation, not checked
  against a published numeric table.
- Чл. 35(1)(6) and 35(5)(2): supervisors/lifeguards must set the day's beach-activity
  schedule for children's groups "съобразно хидрометеорологичните условия" (in accordance
  with weather/sea conditions) and must brief children on "the state of the bottom, the
  degree of swell, and presence of undertow" before swimming — again qualitative,
  judgment-based language, no numeric trigger.
- Приложение № 6 (rescue-station equipment requirements), point 2.2: every rescue station
  must keep on hand reference literature including "скала за определяне силата на вятъра и
  степента на вълнението" — literally "a scale for determining wind force and degree of
  wave/swell" (i.e., a Beaufort-wind-force chart and a sea-state/swell chart). This
  confirms lifeguards *do* use standard meteorological scales (Beaufort for wind, a
  wave/swell degree scale) as *reference tools* for their own judgment — but the ordinance
  never codifies "at Beaufort N, raise flag color X." The mapping from scale-reading to
  flag color is left to the trained lifeguard's discretion.

## On the "1996 predecessor had similarly no numeric thresholds" point (history)

The repealed 1996 ordinance (Наредба за водноспасителната дейност и обезопасяването на
водните площи, приета с ПМС № 182 от 1996 г., обн. ДВ бр. 65/1996, отм. ДВ бр. 30/2024)
defined the same three basic flags — green = "bathing permitted," yellow = "bathing
permitted with increased caution," red = "bathing forbidden" — again with no meteorological
numeric thresholds specified in the text, per the CIELA state-gazette archive summary of
that superseded instrument:
https://www.ciela.net/svobodna-zona-darjaven-vestnik/document/-550469119/issue/7114/naredba-za-vodnospasitelnata-deynost-i-obezopasyavaneto-na-vodnite-ploshti-ot-1996-g
So this is not a recent gap — Bulgarian beach-flag law has never codified numeric
wave/wind thresholds, across at least two successive regulatory generations (1996→2024).

## Unverified / lower-confidence secondary claims (do not treat as legal fact)

These appeared in tourism/blog sources during this research and are **not** corroborated
by the primary ordinance text above. They may reflect informal industry practice, a
different (municipal or resort-internal) set of house rules, or simply be wrong/invented
by the source. Flagging them explicitly per your instructions rather than silently
dropping them:

- A hotel blog (hotelemilia.bg) claimed the green flag requires "swell below 1-2 Beaufort,"
  yellow "2-3 Beaufort," and red "waves exceeding 3-4 metres" — I could not find this
  numeric mapping anywhere in the actual ordinance (current or repealed), and 3-4 m waves
  would be an extraordinarily severe/rare Black Sea storm state, which makes me suspect
  this figure is either a garbled/hallucinated paraphrase or describes some other body's
  internal guidance, not codified law. Treat as unverified.
  https://hotelemilia.bg/en/what-to-know-about-swimming-at-the-beach/
- A Bulgarian National Radio (BNR) Burgas regional item quotes a water-rescue-service
  official (Petar Rusev) discussing a red flag being raised due to "вълнение до 3 бала"
  (swell up to force/grade 3) plus "дънни ями" (bottom pits/holes) on a specific day in
  Burgas — this reads as a lifeguard's real-time discretionary call on a specific date,
  consistent with the "discretion, not fixed rule" conclusion above, but I was unable to
  fetch the full article text (TLS certificate error on old-news.bnr.bg) to confirm exact
  wording/context. URL for follow-up: https://bnr.bg/burgas/post/101158978/
- Various consumer-facing "what do beach flags mean" articles (az-deteto.bg, actualno.com,
  chr.bg, flashnews.bg, paradisodreamshotel.com, varna24.bg) restate the same
  green/yellow/red/red-red/checkered meanings found in the ordinance's Annex 2, plus an
  informal "double red flag" and "brown flag" (pollution) and "purple flag" (dangerous
  marine life) that do **not** appear anywhere in the current ordinance's 5-flag Annex 2.
  These may be older conventions, resort-specific extras, or conflated with
  international/other-country flag systems. Do not encode them as if they were Bulgarian
  law without further primary-source confirmation.

## Implication for the Green Flags rule engine

Given the above, a rule engine that claims to derive Bulgaria's official flag color from
wave height / wind speed thresholds would be **inventing** a threshold that does not exist
in Bulgarian law. Two honest options:
1. Build the deterministic rule engine on your *own* chosen, clearly-labeled thresholds
   (e.g. a defensible synthesis of Beaufort-scale / WMO sea-state conventions), and market
   it explicitly as "our own safety estimate," not as "the official flag" — since no
   Bulgarian authority publishes the latter as a formula.
   uses the Beaufort/sea-state reference tools already required to be on hand at
   every station.

Either way, the product copy should not claim the numeric thresholds are "the law" —
that would be factually false based on this research.
